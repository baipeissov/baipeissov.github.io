Does a Speaking Scorer Trained on Speak & Improve Transfer to IELTS-Style Learner Speech?

Revised draft, November 2026. Work in progress.

Paper (PDF)

Abstract

Automated speaking assessment systems are usually tested on data from the same exam they were trained on. We ask what happens when the test format changes. We fine-tune a 3B-parameter language model on ASR transcripts from the Speak & Improve Corpus 2025 (S&I) and apply it, with no adaptation, to 45 learner recordings from the Chen (2022) English Speech Corpus, which carry IELTS Speaking bands from 5.0 to 8.0. The ranking transfers well: Spearman's ρ is 0.80 (95% CI 0.62–0.91, n=45), compared with 0.71 for single S&I part responses in domain. Absolute agreement does not transfer cleanly. Predictions are compressed towards the middle of the scale, so weaker learners are over-scored by about 0.7–0.8 band and the top band is under-scored by about 1.1, and the size of the offset depends on how the S&I scale is aligned with IELTS. The test set is small and the IELTS labels have unconfirmed provenance, so we treat the results as an initial estimate.

To see why the ranking transfers, we compare it with a BERT baseline grader, split results by part and response length, and add audio-based and pause-based scores. The BERT baseline transfers much worse (ρ=0.46) and mostly tracks length. A frozen WavLM audio model helps in domain, where text plus audio beats text alone, but alone it does not transfer to IELTS (ρ=0.50); pause and fluency features do (ρ=0.74). A small audio-and-fluency network trained from scratch on S&I matches the LLM in domain (ρ=0.73 vs 0.71) and, with the LLM score, improves on it (0.75); on IELTS it reaches 0.68 alone and 0.80 stacked, but it needed all 11,497 training responses to do so (0.36 with 2,907). No combination is distinguishable from the text scorer on 45 recordings.

On a larger second test set (OpenEnded, 1,000 human-rated open responses from 250 speakers), the S&I audio model ranks speakers with ρ≈0.68, and pronunciation (goodness-of-pronunciation) features add a small but reliable gain (0.664 to 0.679); on S&I and Chen they add nothing. A larger encoder (WavLM-large) improves the audio score on both S&I (0.707 to 0.729) and OpenEnded (0.682 to 0.700). The text scorer transfers less well to these delivery-focused ratings (ρ=0.58).
